01 / Problem context

Why practise customer conversations?

Customer-facing employees may encounter emotionally charged, complex and time-sensitive situations.

Failed transactions

Explain checks and next steps while a customer is worried.

Suspected fraud

Respond calmly, protect the customer and follow procedure.

Inaccessible services

Adapt explanations and channels to different needs.

Complex charges

Make fees and processes clear without jargon.

02 / Research question

The problem to investigate

CENTRAL PROBLEM

Bank employees may lack sufficient opportunities to practise challenging customer interactions in realistic, repeatable and low-risk environments before handling similar situations in the workplace.

This may affect preparedness, communication and consistency in complaint handling. Research is required to establish the extent of this training gap and its contribution to customer-service challenges.

Investigate the training gap; do not assume it explains every complaint.

03 / Human-centered design

Discover before designing

01 · EMPATHISE

Interview employees, supervisors and customers. Explore difficult interactions, training experiences and access needs.

02 · DEFINE

Analyse interviews, observations and anonymised complaint categories. Separate skill gaps from outages, staffing, policy and delays.

03 · IDEATE

Compare role-play, instructor simulations, interactive video, desktop simulation and VR.

04 · PROTOTYPE

Build one small virtual branch and one or two service scenarios.

05 · TEST

Measure performance and usability; gather feedback and revise.

These are planned research activities. No interviews or findings are presented as completed.

08 / Feasibility assessment

A limited prototype is feasible—with guardrails

TECHNICAL

Small 3D branch and branching cases are achievable with Unity or Unreal. Defer complex dialogue AI and speech recognition.

FINANCIAL

Headsets, computers, development and maintenance cost money. Pilot with one headset; obtain real quotes before budgeting.

OPERATIONAL

Schedule sessions and guide trainers on score interpretation. Use fictional scenarios approved by banking professionals.

USER + ACCESSIBILITY

Comfort varies. Offer subtitles, adjustable text, accessible controls and desktop alternative; test users with varied needs and digital confidence.

ETHICAL + LEGAL

Fictional data, informed consent, minimal records, secure access and human review. Check applicable Ugandan data-protection requirements.

OVERALL

A small prototype is potentially achievable. A multi-bank AI VR platform needs more technical validation, funding and institutional approval.

09 / Minimum viable product

Keep version one small and testable

IN THE FIRST PROTOTYPE

  • One virtual bank branch
  • Angry customer: failed transaction
  • Customer needs banking charges explained
  • Basic animated characters and simple choices
  • Scoring: empathy, clarity, professionalism, procedural accuracy
  • Simple performance report with improvement areas
  • Desktop fallback without a headset

DEFER UNTIL LATER

  • Multiple branches and bank-specific deployments
  • Complex AI dialogue and advanced speech recognition
  • Live banking-system integration
  • Real customer records
  • Automated employment decisions
Less development risk; learn from users earlier.

11 / 13
11 / Responsible AI

AI may support practice; humans retain responsibility

FAIRNESS + TRANSPARENCY

Review scenarios and scores for bias. Explain when AI is used and what it evaluates.

CONSENT + MINIMISATION

Explain purpose and use; collect only necessary practice data.

SECURITY + ACCESS RIGHTS

Protect assessment records; limit and manage who can see them.

HUMAN OVERSIGHT

Trainers interpret feedback and evaluate employee competence.

OBSERVABLE CRITERIA

Score decisions, approved procedures and consistent rubric items.

FUTURE POSSIBILITY

AI could enable flexible conversations and tailored practice feedback after validation.

Never automate promotion, dismissal or discipline. Emotion detection is not an objective empathy score.
